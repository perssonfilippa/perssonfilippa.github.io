Claude Potate

Business card and logo for an exclusive sofa manufacturer, made in the course Visual Design.

A group project where we designed a business card and logo for the company Claude Potate. The challenge was to communicate the brand’s identity only through the visual expression, without directly writing out the profession or type of business.

We worked with a dark color palette, high contrast and texture to create an exclusive and well-thought-out feeling. Through a clear typographic hierarchy, the logo is the main focus while the contact information has a more secondary role. To create symmetry and visual balance, the elements were centered. We also worked with visual symbolism, where the C and P look like they are “one”, connected by the thin line between them.

The final result was a consistent and modern visual identity for Claude Potate, which is actually a manufacturer of exclusive sofas.

Rebranding

Rebranding of GetYourGuide’s website through new colors and imagery, as part of the course Visual Design.

A group project where the focus was to explore the possibilities and limitations of colors and images.

Starting from a platform of our choice, we chose to redesign GetYourGuide by only updating the color palette and visual style of the images to make it feel more inspiring and personal, without changing the layout or typography. The colors were chosen to inspire people to travel while also creating a harmonious experience for the user. We combined light blue and orange, where blue contributes to a feeling of safety and trust, while orange adds creativity and encouragement according to color psychology.

For the visual style, we replaced well-edited advertising photos with more authentic and realistic images that users could have taken themselves. This makes the experience more relatable and shows what the destinations actually look like in real life.

The final result was a visual improvement that replaces stiff advertising images with real travel inspiration, where color and images work together to create curiosity and engagement for the user.

herMIND

Interactive prototype for the app herMIND, promoting women’s mental health. Designed together with participants in the course Participatory Design.

A project within the course Participatory Design, based on the UN Sustainable Development Goals. As a group, we chose to focus on 3.4 (Ensure healthy lives and promote well-being for all at all ages) and 5.6 (Ensure universal access to sexual and reproductive health).

Together with a participant group consisting of young women, we identified a clear lack of reliable and collected information about women’s mental health in a digital environment filled with uncertain information and paywalls. Together with the participants, we explored the need for a digital solution that is not based on the menstrual cycle, making it more inclusive for women who use contraceptives.

Through a series of workshops and prototype tests, we developed the app prototype herMIND. The app combines science-based information with a mood tracker, statistics and a safe forum for sharing experiences. It was designed with a warm color palette to create a feeling of safety and comfort. The result was a concrete and interactive prototype that addresses an identified gap in the market without commercial barriers.

The project also highlights the design decisions and trade-offs that can occur in participatory design when the user's wishes need to be balanced with the long-term usability of the system.

Sync

Sync is a meeting service meant to replace unnecessary business trips, making digital meetings feel more engaging and real.

To make digital meetings a more engaging and realistic alternative to short business trips, we developed the prototype for the service Sync. The solution aims to reduce the mental fatigue and cognitive load that often occur during video meetings by increasing engagement and supporting the social interaction within the group.

The center of the service is an AI facilitator that analyzes the flow of the conversation in real time to detect when the energy is dropping or when the discussion gets stuck. Instead of taking over the meeting, the AI acts as a supportive tool that gives suggestions, such as starting a poll, creating smaller discussion groups, or reminding the group to take a much-needed coffee break. The interface is also designed to involve the users more actively. To create a sense of safety and trust, the service was designed based on ethical guidelines with a focus on transparency and privacy. The participants always have the final say and remain in control of all suggestions.

The result was an ethically grounded meeting concept where AI supports human interaction in the background. This creates more engaging digital meetings while also contributing to Stockholm’s climate goals.

Dungeon Master

Chat interface for an AI-powered Game Master, made in the course Human–AI Interaction Design.

A design created during a lab in the course Human–AI Interaction Design, focusing on designing an interface where AI acts as an active collaborator. The task was to create an AI-powered Game Master for Dungeons & Dragons, with the goal of making it easy and fun for the user to play together with the AI.

Based on research about the game and its visual style, the entire chat interface was designed from scratch. The AI was set up to respond in an exciting and engaging way. To help the player, suggestions were added to the chat field to show what they could write. It was also made easy to ask for a new response if the player was not happy with the AI’s suggestion.

The result was an interactive prototype where the player and AI create a story together in real time, through an interface that is easy to understand and control.

ANDRUM

An inviting park bench for spontaneous breaks, developed as a physical and digital prototype.

A project that started in the course User Studies in Interaction Design which focuses on the need for recovery in a busy society. The project was carried out as a fictional collaboration with Skanska and the City of Stockholm. Based on Value Sensitive Design (VSD), interviews were conducted with professionals and students around Humlegården. The study showed that although many people value peace and quiet, they find it difficult to prioritize breaks in a busy everyday life filled with work or studies. Fresh air, movement and music were identified as important parts of relaxation. These insights led to the idea of creating an inviting park bench for spontaneous breaks.

The concept was then developed into a physical and digital prototype as part of the course Prototyping in Interaction Design II. The physical bench combines steel and wood with features that support accessibility and safety. The prototype also includes Arduino-controlled lighting and a SwiftUI app with weather data.

The final result was a complete design system that invites people to slow down, recharge and reconnect with their surroundings in an urban environment, based on the needs and wishes of the users.

Micki Glassbil

A wooden toy concept developed in a fictional collaboration with Micki Leksaker.

The project was carried out as a product development project in a fictional collaboration with the toy manufacturer Micki Leksaker. The starting point was Micki's long tradition of manufacturing wooden toys that encourage playful learning and creativity. The challenge was to create a new product concept that preserves the company's classic craftsmanship, while meeting today's demands for sustainable material choices, educational value and modern children. The purpose was to combine an aesthetic design with a functional and safe construction.

To reach the concept, an iterative design process was applied. The work began with a pilot study and target audience analysis to map trends and user needs. Based on these insights, a specification of requirements was established that took safety standards, material choices and manufacturing conditions into account. This was followed by idea generation and sketching where different concepts were explored. Finally, the most promising idea was modeled in the 3D-CAD tool Inventor.

The work resulted in a well-developed toy concept adapted for Micki's product range, where the final result was presented through a polystyrene foam prototype in a 1:1 scale, along with technical documentation consisting of CAD models, drawings, and material specifications. The product meets high standards for both manufacturability and safety, while offering children an engaging play experience.
